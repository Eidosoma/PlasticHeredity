# Cluster Report: Plastic Heredity

## Cluster scope

`HEREDITY` covers S19-L38 through L47. Its central shift was from predicting arrival at a destination to predicting a process that can be certified online. Candidate processes included temporally separated recurrence, inherited parent–daughter similarity, sustained inheritance streaks, disruption and recovery, repeated return, and restoration of functional organization.

The cluster's main scientific distinction is:

- **persistence:** continued parent–daughter similarity without disturbance;
- **restorative recovery:** return toward the old composition after a genuine departure;
- **plastic renewal:** a break in heredity followed by formation of a new hereditary episode, potentially around a different composition.

## Process definitions and controls

Inheritance at a fission boundary was operationalized by strict historical cosine `H>0.9` between parent and selected daughter. A valid process could not be ordinary adjacent smoothness alone. Outcomes used bounded future fission horizons and kept molecular and generational clocks separate. Controls preserved opportunity count, marginal inheritance frequency, event counts, current streak, phase, and chronology where appropriate.

Empirical process probabilities were measured with frozen branch ensembles. Reliability used independent branch halves, corrected between-state variance, intermediate-probability counts, and 4,096 catalytic-matrix bootstraps. Temporal-order tests compared realized order with exact fixed-count permutations. Markov/IID comparisons were cross-fitted.

## From recurrence to disruption and renewal

L38 found that a first prospective recurrence/inheritance family did not yield a reliable committor across all required cohorts. L39 showed that a three-in-a-row inheritance event was common largely because ordinary inheritance itself was common. L40–L41 found no robust evidence that leave–return or repeated-return order supplied a general additional signal.

L42 provided the decisive separation. Generic inheritance resumption after a break occurred in approximately 0.88–0.90 of branches, but strict return to the old compositional neighbourhood occurred in only about 0.0024–0.0094. L43 converted exact recovery into a continuous old-anchor gain. Mean gain at heredity resumption was consistently negative, about −0.26 across development, validation, confirmation, and both candidates.

![Continuous similarity gain toward the old pre-break composition at heredity resumption.](figures/heredity_l43_recovery_gain.png)

*Figure HEREDITY-1. L43 continuous recovery gain. Every cohort/candidate distribution lies well below zero: when heredity resumes, the daughter lineage is generally farther from the old compositional anchor, not restored toward it. Frequent resumption therefore reflects plastic renewal rather than homeostasis to a fixed composition.*

## Seven-outcome process-family audit

L44 jointly analyzed 35,840 frozen F12 branch futures and seven distinct outcomes:

1. inheritance break with departure;
2. generic resumption;
3. a new three-fission hereditary episode;
4. a persistent five-fission hereditary episode;
5. old-neighbourhood recovery;
6. positive old-anchor gain;
7. repeated cross-generation return.

Break probability was about 0.64–0.73, three-fission episode probability about 0.76–0.82, and five-fission episode probability about 0.53–0.60. Exact old-neighbourhood return remained near zero, and positive old-anchor gain stayed around 0.08–0.10.

![Branch probabilities for seven separately defined heredity processes.](figures/heredity_l44_process_prevalence.png)

*Figure HEREDITY-2. L44 process-family prevalence across validation/confirmation and both candidates. The large separation between break/resumption/episode events and old-return events demonstrates that they are not interchangeable labels for one phenomenon.*

The three- and five-fission episodes contained temporal structure beyond the same count of inherited fissions randomly ordered. For run 3, order excess was 0.026–0.032; for run 5, 0.043–0.060, with bootstrap intervals above zero. Cross-fitted first-order Markov models improved over IID inheritance by 0.015–0.022 bits per transition. Inheritance following an inherited boundary was approximately 0.83–0.86, versus 0.66–0.70 after a noninherited boundary. Inherited runs had median dwell 3 fissions; noninherited runs had median dwell 1.

These are modest but consistent signs of sticky hereditary regimes. They are not proof of collective memory, error correction, or biological individuality.

## Information and functional follow-ups

L45 asked whether past-only Phi/PhiID adds held-out value beyond direct process variables such as current H, inheritance frequency, current streak, opportunity count, mass, and phase. It did not. Completed-fit quantities remained future-dependent. This constrains the original paper-related information interpretation without negating the process result.

L46 examined whether renewed hereditary episodes restore a functional pattern despite changing molecular composition. Local post-break regimes showed descriptive coherence in catalytic activation and expected exchange. L47 then conditioned on composition and chronology. The registered functional excess was near zero or negative, and confidence intervals did not establish an independent functional memory.

## Supported, null, and constraining findings

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| Parent–daughter compositional inheritance is common | Supported descriptively |
| Inherited episodes are temporally sticky beyond an IID frequency baseline | Directionally supported across cohorts/candidates |
| Heredity usually resumes after a break | Supported descriptively |
| The old molecular composition is restored | Contradicted; exact return is extremely rare and continuous gain is negative |
| Three- and five-fission episodes contain ordering beyond count | Supported within operational definitions |
| Past-only PhiID adds beyond direct heredity controls | Not supported |
| Functional organization is restored beyond composition/chronology | Not supported |
| These processes constitute biological replication or an organism | Not established |

## Scientific interpretation

The reconstructed chemistry is better described as **plastic heredity with regime switching** than as the arrival of one stable replicator. It frequently maintains or re-establishes parent–daughter compositional similarity, but a post-break episode generally forms around a different molecular realization. The operative invariant is therefore the capacity for local heredity, not exact molecular identity.

This interpretation also resolves several earlier contradictions. The paper's singular “most recurring composition” is too restrictive for a medium that moves among locally hereditary regimes, while adjacent H alone is too permissive to define an emergence event. The joint event “heredity breaks and then a new three-fission episode forms” creates a bounded, future event that is neither immediate smoothness nor privileged-centroid entry.

## Cluster conclusion

`HEREDITY` supplies the scientifically defensible target later used for confirmation. It is operational, branch-evaluable, and does not inspect the completed test lineage to choose a destination. The evidence supports stochastic renewal of a hereditary capacity; it does not support fixed-composition homeostasis, independent functional restoration, or a PhiID precursor.
