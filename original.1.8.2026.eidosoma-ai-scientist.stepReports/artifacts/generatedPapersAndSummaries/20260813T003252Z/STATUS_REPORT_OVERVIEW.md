# Executive Research Overview

## Bottom line

The completed work began as a forensic replication of a preprint claiming that Phi-r/PhiID dynamics predict the first self-replicator in GARD and that Phi-directed molecular edits control replication. After 92 execution-level research steps and one S19 umbrella record, the evidence supports a more selective conclusion:

1. **The simulator and much of the public information-analysis lineage were reconstructed reproducibly.**
2. **Several paper-like spike and association directions occur, but the closest matches are completed-fit, label-coupled, and retrospective.**
3. **The paper's first-quarter prediction and max/control/min causal-control results were not supported within the tested reconstruction.**
4. **A completed-run attractor can have a reliable committor, yet that attractor is not a stable destination across independent lineages.**
5. **The more defensible dynamical object is plastic heredity:** parent–daughter heredity breaks, then a new locally hereditary episode forms without returning to the old composition.
6. **A frozen, target-blind full-state graph-plus-history coordinate predicts the independently measured probability of that future break-and-renewal event on untouched matrices in both simulator candidates.**

The final positive result is not PhiID support, paper replication, a first-replicator detector, an intervention result, or evidence outside the reconstructed simulator.

## Anchor results

### Forensic replication verdict

The immutable S18 claim matrix classified 3 of 59 claims as supported, 17 as directionally supported, 21 as not supported within tested scope, 2 as underdetermined, and 16 as not evaluated. Figure 2-like excursions and Figures 3–4-like associations were directionally present. The aggregate Figure 2 trend, Figure 5 prediction advantage, and Figure 6/Table 1 causal ordering were not reproduced. The completed-fit Phi branch used the whole trajectory and failed the interpretation required for early warning.

### Attractor/committor separation

At L28, independent 64-branch halves produced split-half Spearman correlations of 0.926 and 0.933 for a 32-observation attractor-entry committor. An eight-step stochastic propagator later predicted that committor on untouched matrices (Spearman 0.900 and 0.821). But independent lineages failed strict basin and atlas transfer. The signal was real; the named destination was not general.

### Plastic heredity

Across L44 validation and confirmation cohorts, inheritance break probabilities were roughly 0.64–0.73 and resumption given break roughly 0.88–0.91. Old-neighbourhood recovery-event prevalence was about 0.0026–0.0069 in the reliability summaries, while the separate conditional-on-break plasticity table gave about 0.0024–0.0051; continuous similarity to the old anchor decreased by about 0.26 at resumption. Three- and five-fission inherited episodes occurred more often than exact count-matched temporal permutations, while first-order Markov models modestly outperformed IID inheritance-frequency baselines.

### Confirmed process-risk coordinate

L54 used 40 untouched shared catalytic matrices, 80 trajectories, 400 post-fission states, and 25,600 independent F12 branches plus an exact second campaign. Empirical probability split-half Spearman was 0.938/0.924. The frozen model's overall q ranks were 0.895–0.918 and centered within-matrix ranks 0.550–0.697. Proper-score improvements over direct history had positive 95% lower bounds in all registered candidate/direction comparisons; every whole-matrix permutation test returned `p=0.001949`. All replay and integrity gates passed.

## What is supported

- Named GARD simulation branches can be implemented, validated, and replayed.
- Completed-fit public PhiRL values can show small paper-like retrospective associations with an exact-H label.
- A finite-horizon transition probability can be measured reliably by branching a restored state.
- The original completed-lineage attractor target is not stable across independent lineages.
- The reconstructed medium exhibits frequent local compositional heredity, disruption, and plastic renewal.
- A frozen past-observable state/network/history coordinate predicts the future plastic-heredity transition probability in both simulator candidates on new matrices.

## What is not supported

- Identification of the authors' exact simulator, label, Phi scalar, prediction tensor, or intervention scorer.
- A coherent public pipeline reproducing all paper figures and Table 1.
- Prospective PhiID prediction of first-replicator appearance.
- Incremental past-only PhiID information beyond direct stability or heredity controls.
- Max/control/min bidirectional causal control.
- One privileged or cross-lineage-stable compositional attractor.
- Restoration of the old molecular composition or independent restored function.
- Biological heredity, agency, or origin-of-life validity outside the simulator.

## Research-program status

The group plan contains seven serial Experiments and 112 originally planned research steps. E01, forensic replication, is complete and deterministically closed. It generated a validated simulation/evidence foundation, a constraining paper verdict, and the additive L54 process-risk confirmation. E02's first stage is authorized but unexecuted; it must treat both PhiID and the L54 coordinate as nonprivileged candidates and attempt to falsify them through leakage, calibration, robustness, target sensitivity, and simpler-control comparisons. E03–E07—regime discovery, causal boundaries, regenerative memory, minimal competency, and collective organization—remain future work and depend on E02's adjudication.

## Recommended next action

Proceed only with the separately versioned E02 adversarial validation. Preserve S18 and L54 immutably. The central test is whether the L54 coordinate survives target, preprocessing, numerical, and simulator sensitivity and adds value beyond exact H, direct history, ordinary stability, phase, and matrix-level propensity. Intervention work should remain inactive until that falsification succeeds.
