# Strict-eight mechanism diagnosis — lay summary

Strict eight is a sequence of hurdles: the lineage must first leave its old
form, later produce eight inherited parent-to-selected-daughter steps, keep all
eight daughters mutually coherent, and remain separated from the old anchor.
We analyzed these hurdles separately rather than treating the rare final label
as one indivisible outcome.

Starting concentration and detailed state information consistently helped
predict whether a break would occur. They were much less consistent for the
later coherence hurdle and did not robustly predict old-anchor separation.
This explains part of the earlier prediction problem: the information is
stage-specific, while the complete event combines several differently
predictable requirements.

We then changed concentration and occupied-type richness directly. Across
1,408,000 fresh futures, neither making compositions more concentrated versus
flatter nor contracting versus expanding their occupied types increased the
complete strict-eight event. None of the 24 primary causal cells passed. The
richness edit's largest localized effect actually reduced the chance of
reaching a later inherited run of eight.

Concentration is therefore an informative marker—especially for break risk—
but the tested generic diversity changes are not a demonstrated strict-eight
mechanism. The more plausible missing variable is which molecules are present
and how their composition fits the catalytic matrix, rather than diversity
alone.

This is a post-hoc diagnosis on surviving, observable, selected-lineage states.
It narrows interpretation but does not change the original strict-eight
occurrence result.
