# Suggested manuscript and reviewer-response language

**Status:** post-hoc internal mechanism diagnosis  
**Fresh outcomes:** 1,408,000 intervention futures from retained states  
**Use:** supplementary Results and limitations; not a new strict-eight headline

## Supplementary Methods

> We decomposed the strict coherent-eight endpoint into four ordered gates:
> first break, a later inherited run of eight, mutual coherence conditional on
> that run, and old-anchor separation conditional on coherence. Development-
> only binomial models compared ten-boundary history, six exact concentration
> summaries, and the retained state block; models were sealed before
> confirmation scoring. Parallel continuous-margin models and branch-budget
> reliability analyses distinguished weak prediction from finite-future label
> noise. Separately, 2,000 retained confirmation states received no-op and
> common-random-stream one- or four-molecule edits that altered evenness or
> occupied-type richness over 32 future fissions. These analyses were designed
> after the strict-event result and are post hoc.

## Results: stage-resolved prediction

> The strict endpoint did not behave as one uniformly predictable outcome.
> Adding concentration summaries beyond history improved first-break
> prediction in all 12 endpoint-by-candidate-by-half cells (mean held-out gain
> 0.00788 nats), as did adding the retained state block (mean gain 0.00836
> nats). For coherence conditional on a run of eight, concentration passed 4/12
> cells and state passed 6/12; neither pattern was uniform across endpoint
> metrics and implementations. No model contrast robustly predicted old-anchor
> separation. These results preserve the failed prospective full-event verdict
> while locating most reproducible state-local information at the break gate.

## Results: generic diversity interventions

> Directly changing generic concentration or occupied-type richness did not
> increase complete strict-eight occurrence. Concentrating rather than
> flattening the starting composition had a mean event-rate effect of -0.00111
> and passed 0/12 cells. Contracting rather than expanding occupied richness
> had a mean effect of -0.00056 and passed 0/12 cells. Across the two contrasts,
> 0/24 strict-event cells met the frozen causal rule. The largest localized
> mean effect was a -0.0211 change in run-of-eight recovery for richness
> contraction versus expansion, opposite a simple “lower diversity creates
> heredity” account.

## Discussion

> Concentration is therefore a reproducible marker of where strict-event risk
> enters the trajectory, especially at the first-break gate, but the tested
> concentration and richness axes are not demonstrated causes of the complete
> event. This distinction is consistent with the matched non-degeneracy audit:
> strict windows are more concentrated than hard same-state controls, yet
> generic concentration edits do not recreate them. Composition identity and
> its relation to the catalytic matrix remain more plausible targets than
> diversity alone.

## Limitations

> These analyses reuse surviving, observable landmark states and follow one
> selected daughter. Later conditional gates have fewer eligible successes and
> failures than the break gate, and some cells lack the frozen power required
> for a mechanistic conclusion. A null intervention result applies only to the
> exact one- and four-molecule policies and may reflect either a genuinely
> irrelevant axis or an effect too small for this rare endpoint and branch
> budget. No result licenses selection of the most favorable endpoint metric or
> isolated candidate cell.

## Reviewer response

> We added a post-hoc stage-resolved diagnosis and fresh diversity
> interventions. Concentration and retained state information robustly
> predicted the first-break gate, but later coherence and anchor gates were not
> uniformly predicted. Neither concentration/flattening nor richness
> contraction/expansion increased complete strict-eight occurrence in any
> frozen primary cell. We therefore describe concentration as an associated
> feature of strict-event geometry, not as an established causal mechanism.
