# Suggested manuscript and reviewer-response language

**Status:** optional post-hoc mechanistic follow-up  
**Fresh outcomes:** 896,000 intervention futures from retained confirmation states  
**Confirmatory status:** none relative to the original strict-eight claim

This analysis was prospectively frozen before its new intervention futures,
but the hypothesis was developed after the original strict-eight result. It
must be called post hoc, not preregistered confirmation. No `NewIdeas` artifact
was an input.

## Supplementary Methods: beta-derived rulebook

> As a post-hoc mechanistic follow-up, we derived a deterministic composition-
> space reference directly from each frozen catalytic matrix and the
> simulator's expected joining-minus-leaving equations. Sixteen fixed-seed
> simplex starts were iterated to a normalized expected-flow fixed point and
> solutions within cosine 0.95 were merged. The derivation was outcome blind.
> For each retained state, ten features summarized distance to the nearest
> beta-derived form, state and form self-support, local expected-flow direction
> and magnitude, one-step alignment gain, tangent-space stability, and the
> form's diversity and leading-species share.

## Supplementary Methods: transferable holding score

> To test whether strict-event propensity was a matrix-level property rather
> than a trajectory-specific fluctuation, each target state received a cross-
> fitted holding score computed only from the opposite candidate under the
> same catalytic matrix, pooling its five landmarks and using the opposite
> 64-branch half. Development matrices alone selected and fitted the binomial
> ridge models; confirmation was scored once without recalibration. Because
> this score uses sibling futures, it is a mechanistic matrix calibration and
> not a launch-time predictor available from one untouched assembly.

## Results: held-out prediction

> Under the registered cosine endpoint, adding deterministic beta-derived
> rulebook features beyond history, six concentration summaries, and the
> retained state block improved held-out log loss by 0.04708 nats for mutual
> coherence conditional on a run of eight and by 0.00909 nats for the complete
> strict event; all four candidate-by-half cells passed the frozen post-hoc
> evidence rule. Adding the opposite-candidate holding score to the same base
> improved the two targets by 0.05744 and 0.01340 nats, again passing all four
> cells. Direct matrix-level cross-candidate Spearman correlations averaged
> 0.913 for coherence conditional on a run and 0.796 for strict-eight
> occurrence. These results identify transferable held-out information; they
> do not make the sibling-future score a prospective single-state instrument.

## Supplementary Methods: alignment intervention

> Each of the 2,000 retained confirmation states was assigned seven frozen
> arms: no-op; one- and four-molecule transfers toward the nearest beta-derived
> form; matched transfers away from it; and random transfers at the same two
> doses. Every transfer moved a molecule between already occupied types,
> preserving total mass and occupied-type count. Competing arms shared 64
> future random streams and a 32-fission horizon. Primary registered-cosine
> outcomes separated a break within the first eight fissions, eight
> uninterrupted inherited boundaries, and uninterrupted inheritance plus
> mutual coherence. The complete strict-eight event was a secondary,
> direction-agnostic readout because it requires both a break and later
> stabilization.

## Results: alignment intervention

> Moving four molecules toward rather than away from the beta-derived form
> increased the joint eight-boundary-plus-mutual-coherence outcome in all four
> candidate-by-half cells. Absolute effects ranged from +0.00775 to +0.00909
> (mean +0.00820), raising the outcome from approximately 5.2% to 6.0%; every
> whole-matrix interval was positive. The manipulation did not produce a
> supported effect on first break, eight uninterrupted inherited boundaries,
> or complete strict-eight occurrence. Much of the coherence contrast arose
> because away edits reduced coherence relative to no-op, while toward edits
> produced a smaller improvement. Alignment remained clearly separated after
> one and four fissions, was small by fission eight, and was unresolved by
> fission 32.

> The frozen global causal gate was not met. Candidate 02 achieved the complete
> four-molecule toward/away edit in 91.0% of states, whereas candidate 03
> achieved it in 89.7%, narrowly below the registered 90% feasibility rule.
> The candidate-03 effect estimates and intervals remained positive, but those
> two cells could not receive a formal pass; the endpoint therefore passed 2/4
> cells and the all-cell claim gate remained false.

## Discussion

> Together with the stage-resolved diagnosis, these post-hoc results suggest
> that strict eight contains separable processes. Beta-conditioned
> compositional alignment carried transferable information and affected mutual
> coherence among eight selected descendants, but it did not control the
> initial break or increase the complete event. The result is therefore
> consistent with a matrix-structured coherence or capture component, not with
> a solved break–capture–hold mechanism or a permanent composition-space
> attractor.

## Limitations

> This post-hoc follow-up reuses surviving, observable confirmation states and
> follows one selected daughter per fission. The empirical holding score uses
> opposite-candidate futures and is not available prospectively from a single
> state. The intervention preserves mass and occupied-type count but does not
> hold every evenness statistic fixed. Its complete strict-eight readout was
> null, and the global coherence gate was withheld because two feasibility
> cells fell 0.3 percentage points below the frozen threshold. Causal language
> is limited to the exact molecule-transfer policies in this simulator.

## Reviewer response

> We added a post-hoc mechanistic analysis that derives a matrix-specific
> reference composition directly from the frozen simulator equations and then
> tests both held-out prediction and fresh, mass- and occupied-set-preserving
> interventions. The matrix-derived and cross-candidate holding variables
> improved held-out prediction of coherence and strict-eight occurrence beyond
> the retained history/concentration/state base. Four-molecule alignment edits
> causally shifted eight-way coherence in the expected direction, but did not
> increase complete strict-eight occurrence. We therefore narrowed the
> interpretation to a matrix-structured coherence component and explicitly
> report that the frozen all-cell causal gate was not met.

## Do not write

- “The catalytic rulebook causes strict eight.”
- “Strict-eight prediction is now prospectively confirmed.”
- “The intervention established a permanent attractor or memory.”
- “All four causal cells passed.”
- “The cross-candidate score is available from one launch state.”
