# Plastic-heredity replication results ledger

**Status:** 15 August 2026
**Purpose:** one chronological, human-readable record of the original clean-room replication, the fivefold rerun, the review-driven corrections, the completed strict-event pilot and ensemble test, and the current scientific claim boundary.

This ledger summarizes the evidence. The immutable result bundles, CSV files, JSON files, registrations, and checksums linked below remain the numerical sources of record. If a summary here ever differs from a sealed artifact, the sealed artifact controls.

## Current bottom line

The potential discovery survives only at the predictive, composite-algorithm level; its proposed mechanistic decomposition did not survive the strictest correction:

> In two explicit clean-room reconstructions of the GARD simulator, the probability that compositional heredity will break and then renew for three fissions varies reliably across restored states, and the original frozen state/graph/history algorithm predicts that probability better than its registered direct-history comparator. A later no-PCA, provenance-complete prospective ablation did not robustly isolate incremental composition, static-beta, or beta-conditioned-state contributions. A post-hoc audit found that most qualifying three-fission episodes are not one mutually coherent compositional neighbourhood. A subsequent prospective F32 experiment nevertheless confirmed that the simulator sometimes produces the much stricter event of a break followed by a mutually coherent, old-anchor-distinct eight-fission episode, at about 1.8–2.1% of futures. Its registered state-added predictor failed, a six-family follow-up pilot could not stably select one common model, and a separately registered direct-plus-hurdle ensemble passed candidate 03 but failed the required all-candidate gate. Separately, post-break inheritance exhibits out-of-matrix first-order and run-duration predictive dependence.

This does **not** establish biological memory, molecular information storage, error correction, causal control, a universal prebiotic law, or identity with the unavailable original implementation.

## 3. Review of feature confounding and mechanistic ablation

### Reviewer concern

The reviewed old implementation mixed several possible explanations in its full-versus-direct comparison:

- duplicated or affine-equivalent mass, generation, and history directions;
- prior-cycle and cumulative growth clocks absent from the direct baseline;
- L2 regularization geometry that can favor duplicated correlated directions;
- current composition, static network structure, and network-conditioned state.

The original frozen algorithm comparison remained valid, but it could not isolate the stronger mechanistic interpretation.

### Prospective response

Using only the retained 5× development cohort, the response reconstructed and verified all 399 retained feature coordinates and all 64,000 target rows before fitting. It then separated:

- `H8`: unique direct history;
- `H10`: `H8` plus prior-cycle and cumulative growth clocks;
- `S`: mass-free current composition/state;
- `B`: the registered state-free beta/network representation;
- `I`: mass-free network-conditioned state, residualized against `H10 + S + B` on development data;
- `D`: duplicated mass and generation negative controls.

Registration `57e0f9e00da2f5562e6caa2f80e690a9f2fc82f276f139205b6d3d1257f0d112` was sealed before generating MECHCONF.

### MECHCONF primary results

Values are held-out log-loss gains for the two preassigned branch halves.

| Contrast | Candidate 02 A/B | Candidate 03 A/B | Registered outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Current state beyond all-clock history | 0.00646 / 0.00676 | 0.00853 / 0.00726 | **Passed** |

Every passing primary test had a positive 95% lower bound and Holm-adjusted `p=0.00293`. Static network tests had intervals spanning zero and Holm-adjusted `p=0.81328`.

### Diagnostic controls

- Growth clocks mattered: `H8 → H10` gains were 0.00923/0.00759 in candidate 02 and 0.00816/0.00911 in candidate 03.
- Corrected duplicate gains were at most approximately `3.24e-8`.
- Under the same-penalty ridge control, duplicate gains were no larger than approximately `8.54e-6`.
- The original legacy composite still beat its original history baseline on the new cohort by 0.02203/0.02045 in candidate 02 and 0.02587/0.02802 in candidate 03.

### Interpretation at that stage

MECHCONF materially strengthened the evidence relative to the original composite comparison, but its state and interaction attribution was later challenged by the beta-completeness review and superseded by the MECHCONF2 claim boundary described below. At this stage:

- the registered current-composition block carried information beyond unique history plus both growth clocks;
- static catalytic structure alone did not add detectable information in the registered v1 comparison;
- the registered network-conditioned current-state block added information;
- duplicated directions do not explain the observed advantage in our controls;
- prediction does not demonstrate causal control.

## 4. Review of the L44 IID comparator and inheritance dependence

### Reviewer concern

The old L44 calculation fitted its IID probability using every suffix symbol, including first symbols and singleton sequences, but scored IID only on symbols at positions 1 onward. Markov fitting and scoring both used only transitions. This support mismatch allowed unscored observations to alter the IID baseline and made the published 0.015–0.022-bit Markov advantage unreliable.

The L44 sequences are unavailable in this folder. The old number is therefore **withdrawn**, not retroactively repaired. This defect does not affect the L54 process-risk predictor.

### Retrospective diagnostic

The existing 5× L54 confirmation futures were regenerated to quantify the issue under our separate clean-room process. All 128,000 retained branch rows and the original batch digest matched exactly.

| Candidate | Mismatched-IID Markov gain | Corrected Markov gain | Inflation from mismatch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 02 | 0.057430 | 0.056881 | 0.000550 |
| 03 | 0.038494 | 0.038103 | 0.000391 |

This shows the direction and size of the problem only in our L54 cohort. It says nothing conclusive about the unavailable L44 effect.

### Prospective response

Registration `0a100eb3d626f3fdb92f5b4f84f1404b095fc1d21b1dfe6b3a83d2adf0e78f1f` froze the following before MEMCONF:

- sequences consist of observed inheritance symbols strictly after the first break;
- no-break, empty, and singleton suffixes contribute no transitions;
- IID, Markov, and duration-aware models fit and score the identical transition destinations;
- duration bins are fixed at `1, 2, 3, 4, 5+` and use past symbols only;
- every Bernoulli cell uses a Beta(1,1) posterior mean;
- cross-fitting holds out whole catalytic matrices in even-to-odd and odd-to-even directions;
- the primary estimand is transition-weighted held-out bits per transition;
- 4,096 matrix bootstraps, 4,096 whole-matrix sign randomizations, and Holm correction cover the four primary candidate/contrast tests.

### MEMCONF results

| Contrast | Candidate | Gain, bits/transition | 95% CI | Holm p | Both directions positive | Outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Markov over IID | 02 | 0.046953 | [0.039503, 0.056486] | 0.000976 | True | **Passed** |
| Duration-aware over Markov | 02 | 0.010770 | [0.008657, 0.013493] | 0.000976 | True | **Passed** |
| Markov over IID | 03 | 0.033936 | [0.028894, 0.040376] | 0.000976 | True | **Passed** |
| Duration-aware over Markov | 03 | 0.009984 | [0.007921, 0.012333] | 0.000976 | True | **Passed** |

Candidate 02 contributed 1,030,942 scored transitions and candidate 03 contributed 1,095,600. Equal-state macro gains closely matched the transition-weighted gains. First-order gains were positive in 190/200 and 187/200 matrices; duration gains were positive in 181/200 and 177/200 matrices for candidates 02/03, respectively. All 128,000 variable-length futures regenerated exactly.

### Revised interpretation

The simulator exhibits statistically reliable first-order and run-duration predictive dependence after a break. In plain terms, knowing whether the most recent boundary inherited—and how long the current inherited or non-inherited run has lasted—improves prediction of the next boundary on held-out matrices.

Calling this **biological memory** would still be too strong. A pooled duration effect can partly arise from unmodelled differences among catalytic matrices or current molecular states. The next mechanistic memory test must condition on matrix propensity and current composition in another untouched cohort.

Sources: [support-mismatch diagnostic](results/memory_diagnostic/MEMORY_DIAGNOSTIC.md), [sealed protocol](results/memory_registration/MEMORY_REGISTRATION.md), and [MEMCONF result](results/memory_confirmation/MEMORY_RESULTS.md).

## 5. Review of beta completeness and no-PCA mechanistic correction

### Reviewer concern

The old-paper beta-only selector omitted 11 beta-only summaries, and the broader interpretive problem also applied to our v1 ablation: after removing constants and affine duplicates, its 87 beta directions were reduced to 12 unsupervised PCs. Those PCs retained approximately 76% of standardized beta-block variance, so a modest-variance but predictive beta direction could have been lost. The v1 interaction block was residualized against that truncated beta representation. Consequently, MECHCONF supported its registered representations but could not establish that beta was generally uninformative or that the extra signal necessarily required a network–state interaction.

### Prospective correction

The v2 workflow attached explicit state, beta, history, clock, mass, and phase provenance to every coordinate. It added a fixed threshold-free beta panel covering distributions of raw and log beta, row and column strengths and their correlation, reciprocity, normalized asymmetry, the complete 100-value normalized singular spectrum, stable rank, spectral entropy, and strength concentration. No thresholded graph features or supervised feature selection were used.

After development-only removal of constants and exact affine duplicates, 240 beta directions, 11/12 state directions, and all 64 interaction directions remained. None used PCA. Sequential offset-ridge additions preserved the preceding prediction unchanged; penalties were selected on the retained development cohort by deterministic five-fold whole-matrix cross-validation. Registration `7c6acd1e3bac96dae7931c48bff39deedfc6550344dfea3753949956f71701bd` was sealed before the new models were scored on old MECHCONF and before any MECHCONF2 matrix was generated.

The generic simulator manifest retains an unused backward-compatible `pca_components=12` configuration field. The authoritative v2 model contract and audit both record `uses_pca=false`; no v2 added block was PCA-compressed.

The post-hoc old-cohort diagnostic was consistent with failure of the stronger attribution, but it was not used for a claim or to change the protocol.

### MECHCONF2 primary results

Values are held-out log-loss gains for the two preassigned branch halves.

| Contrast | Candidate 02 A/B | Candidate 03 A/B | Registered outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Current state beyond all-clock history | 0.001419 / 0.001282 | 0.002216 / 0.001577 | **Failed** |
| Comprehensive static beta beyond history + state | -0.000226 / 0.000037 | 0.001546 / 0.001475 | **Failed** |
| Beta-conditioned state beyond the comprehensive additive model | -0.009269 / -0.009987 | -0.001738 / -0.001221 | **Failed** |

No contrast passed any complete four-test candidate/half gate. Some individual state or candidate-03 beta intervals were barely positive, but none survived the predeclared Holm family; adjusted values were at least 0.210886. The interaction addition significantly worsened candidate-02 log loss and was negative but inconclusive in candidate 03.

Independent branch-half reliability remained high—0.9260/0.9247 overall and 0.6858/0.6849 after matrix centering in candidates 02/03—so the null attribution result is not explained by an unreliable measured outcome. All 128,000 futures replayed exactly, all saved predictions read back within `1e-12`, and every primary gain independently recomputed within `1e-14`.

### Current interpretation

- The future event still has a reliable, state-indexed probability.
- The original frozen composite algorithm still beats its original direct-history comparator; MECHCONF2 did not retest or invalidate that registered algorithm comparison.
- Growth clocks remain a supported v1 finding.
- Composition beyond clocks is representation-sensitive and is not presently established as a robust mechanistic component.
- No incremental static-beta signal was detected in either registered suite, including the comprehensive no-PCA panel; this remains a representation-specific null.
- A necessary beta-conditioned state interaction is not established and the former stronger interaction wording is withdrawn.

## 6. Review of the “new regime” interpretation

### Reviewer concern

`JOINT_BREAK_RUN3` correctly requires a strict inheritance break followed by three inherited fissions, but it does not require the three episode daughters to be mutually similar, separated from the pre-break composition, recurrent, or persistent beyond three fissions. Adjacent parent-to-daughter cosine similarity is not transitive. The registered target therefore establishes break followed by renewed short-run inheritance, not formation of a distinct new compositional regime.

### Exploratory response

A standalone post-hoc audit regenerated every positive target branch from the three independent 200-matrix F12 confirmation cohorts: scaled5, MECHCONF, and MECHCONF2. All reconstructed states and archived targets matched; all 145,516 positive target and discrete process outcomes replayed exactly, and the largest continuous-process difference was `1.11e-16`.

For the first qualifying episode in each future, the audit measured all pairwise similarities among the three daughters, similarity to the first pre-break parent, uninterrupted run length, same-run persistence to five, and a later break followed by another run of three within F12. The last quantity measures a second renewal, not return to the same composition. Estimates used 4,096 whole-matrix bootstraps over all 200 matrices and were kept separate by cohort, candidate, and branch half. All thresholds were declared post-hoc sensitivities rather than confirmation gates.

Across the six cohort/candidate comparisons:

- mean minimum pairwise daughter similarity was 0.681–0.704;
- only 4.5–6.5% of episodes had every daughter pair above `H=0.9`;
- 93.2–94.9% kept every episode daughter at or below `H=0.9` from the old anchor;
- among episodes with observable five-fission status, 75.9–78.9% continued in the same inherited run to five;
- a later break followed by another observed run of three occurred in 10.2–11.7% of qualifying F12 futures.

### Current interpretation

The episodes are usually compositionally separated from the pre-break parent and often persist when persistence is observable, but they usually do not form one mutually coherent `H>0.9` daughter neighbourhood. This makes **break-and-renewal** the supported description. “New hereditary regime” or “regime switching” remains a proposed dynamical interpretation that requires a frozen coherence/distinctness/persistence endpoint on another untouched cohort.

Source: [post-hoc episode-coherence audit](results/episode_coherence_audit/EPISODE_COHERENCE_AUDIT.md).

## 7. Prospective strict eight-fission episode and completed prediction tests

### Frozen occurrence result

The follow-up campaign replaced the permissive three-adjacent-inheritance interpretation with a prospective F32 endpoint requiring, after the first `H<=0.9` break:

- eight consecutive strict `H>0.9` inheritances;
- strict `H>0.9` similarity for all 28 daughter pairs; and
- inclusive `H<=0.85` similarity between every daughter and the pre-break parent.

The endpoint occurred in both candidates and both fixed branch halves:

| Candidate | Half | Rate | 95% matrix-bootstrap CI | Events | Event matrices |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 02 | A | 0.01869 | [0.01391, 0.02369] | 1,196 | 119 |
| 02 | B | 0.01809 | [0.01387, 0.02283] | 1,158 | 126 |
| 03 | A | 0.02089 | [0.01563, 0.02648] | 1,337 | 129 |
| 03 | B | 0.02109 | [0.01626, 0.02677] | 1,350 | 143 |

The frozen state-added predictor did not pass the complete four-cell gate. Its log-loss gains were `0.000525`, `0.000284`, `0.000388`, and `0.000356`; only candidate-03 half A passed after interval and Holm correction. Prespecified first-five occurrence was 7.14–8.35%, and centroid occurrence was 8.27–9.41%, but neither secondary endpoint may replace the strict primary.

The supported discovery therefore became narrower and more concrete: the simulator can generate a break followed by a distinct, mutually coherent eight-fission hereditary episode. Its probability has not yet been robustly predicted beyond unique all-clock history, and the result does not establish recurrence, an attractor, causal control, biological individuality, or prebiotic realism.

### Read-only diagnosis for the next predictor

The new prediction workflow first decomposes the already-seen strict result without changing it. In REGCONF, breaks occurred in 69.07%/73.86% of candidate-02/03 futures and a later eight-run in 52.74%/56.24%; only 3.49%/3.73% of those eight-runs met the strict geometry. Deterministic 80-of-200-matrix subsampling showed that the old small state-added gain would remain directionally uncertain at pilot scale, particularly in candidate 02. Rebuilding all 2,000 sealed states reproduced h10 and state exactly and beta/interaction within `3.56e-15`, below the `1e-14` audit tolerance. This diagnosis is explicitly post-hoc and cannot choose or validate the next predictor.

### Implemented prospective response

The new isolated workflow adds analytic, molecule-label-invariant summaries of expected drift, event noise, entropy/concentration change, local Jacobian stability, and recent compositional velocity. It records every eligible eight-run window and an exact continuous joint margin, decomposes prediction into break, eight-run, and strict-geometry stages, and compares six fixed families: direct ridge, hurdle, hierarchical offset, local dynamics, leakage-safe auxiliary stacking, and one bounded calibrated nonlinear model.

The scientific sequence was frozen as:

1. 80 new matrices × two candidates × five landmarks × 128 F32 futures, with complete replay;
2. positive strict log-loss gain in both candidates and both fixed branch halves, adequate event counts, a one-standard-error simplicity rule, and at least 75% whole-matrix bootstrap selection stability;
3. stop without confirmation if no model passes; otherwise freeze one common family with candidate-specific parameters;
4. test it once on 200 new matrices and 256,000 new futures, requiring positive gain, a positive matrix-bootstrap lower bound, and Holm-adjusted matrix-randomization `p<0.05` in all four cells.

First-five, centroid, continuous-margin, hurdle-component, and post-break analyses remain secondary. Molecular intervention/control was deferred during this program and is not supported by its completed prediction results.

### Six-family pilot result

The pilot executed correctly on 80 fresh matrices, 800 restored states and 102,400 F32 futures, and every future replayed exactly. Event power was adequate, and four of six families improved strict-event log loss over `h10` in all four candidate/half cells. The registered selection question nevertheless failed: whole-matrix bootstrap wins split mainly between direct ridge at 43.63% and the hurdle model at 55.44%, so neither reached the required 75%. No family was frozen, the registered pilot status was `stopped_before_confirmation`, and the original confirmation command remained unauthorized.

### Separately registered ensemble result

The pilot's equal-probability direct-plus-hurdle ensemble was a post-hoc idea and did not rescue that failure. It was instead frozen in a new registration, with no refitting or recalibration, and tested once on 200 untouched matrices, 2,000 restored states and 256,000 F32 futures. Log-loss gains were directionally positive in all four cells: `0.002121` and `0.001800` in candidate 02, and `0.002859` and `0.003313` in candidate 03. Both candidate-03 intervals excluded zero and passed Holm correction (`p=0.0225`), but both candidate-02 intervals crossed zero and failed (`p=0.1181`). The preregistered all-candidate verdict was therefore `primary_prediction_supported: false`. All futures replayed exactly, and strict-event occurrence was again observed at 0.01710 in candidate 02 and 0.02116 in candidate 03.

The completed tests leave a bounded conclusion: strict-event occurrence is reproducible, and the tested models show candidate-dependent predictive signal, but no tested predictor has passed a common prospective transfer gate across both simulator contracts. Molecular intervention/control remains outside this Codex branch's established evidence.

## Final claim boundary

The strongest current conclusion is simulator-specific and predictive:

> The break-and-renewal event has a reproducible state-indexed probability, the original frozen state/graph/history algorithm predicts it better than its registered direct-history comparator, and post-break inheritance contains first-order and run-duration predictive structure that transfers across held-out catalytic matrices. Separately, prospective F32 experiments confirm rare break-followed-by-distinct-coherent eight-fission episodes, but the registered state-added predictor, the six-family selection pilot and the separately frozen direct-plus-hurdle ensemble all failed to establish prediction across both simulator candidates. The successful original composite has not yet been decomposed into a representation-robust mechanism, and neither result establishes recurrence, attractor switching, or causal control.

The work does not yet show why those relationships arise, that the system stores or retrieves information in a biological sense, or that manipulating the predictors controls the process.
