# PX3 post-hoc audit: lay summary

PX3 asked whether tiny molecular edits could deliberately turn a broad
information score up or down, and whether turning it up also improved
heredity. On the fresh registered test, the supposed "up" edits moved the
score down and slightly reduced heredity. The registered test therefore
failed and remains failed.

The audit found no reversed label or simulator bug. It found that the score
was numerically fragile: development estimated a 200-dimensional covariance
from only 128 transitions, while confirmation used 256. The smaller estimate
was partly constructed from a mathematical stabilizer used where data were
missing. When the same fresh trajectories were measured with 128 transitions,
the trained direction reappeared; with 256 or 512 it reversed. That is a
sample-size artifact, not robust control of a physical quantity.

This does not weaken the separately demonstrated plastic-heredity phenomenon
or its causal steering. It means only that PX3 did not connect those robust
heredity effects to this particular high-dimensional Phi-like score. The
public nine-atom Phi-r reading also remained essentially unchanged.
