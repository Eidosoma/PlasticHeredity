# Reporting boundary for the strict-B follow-up

## Status of this work

This campaign is an **exploratory, reviewer-motivated follow-up**.  It tests
stronger interpretations of rare strict coherent-eight episodes that are not
claims of the current preprint.  In particular, the preprint does not claim
that a post-break form is a stable lineage-specific identity, that every state
under a rule converges to one destination, or that a tested rule supports two
independently transmissible stable forms.

The design and decision gates were frozen before this campaign's new futures,
but that prospective freezing does not convert the campaign into a
preregistration made before the earlier observations.  These results must be
reported as exploratory and must not be presented as a confirmatory test of,
or a failed replication of, the preprint's claims.

## What is and is not replicated

Candidates 02 and 03 are two reconstructed simulator contracts evaluated
inside one follow-up campaign.  Agreement between them is a cross-contract
robustness result.  It is **not an independent scientific replication** of
this transplant experiment.

The full exact replay regenerates the same registered futures and checks their
identity.  Passing it establishes computational reproducibility and artifact
integrity.  It does **not** establish independent replication.

Accordingly, reporting must not use phrases such as “the transplant result
was independently replicated” or “two replications failed heredity.”  The
accurate description is “one exploratory stress test gave concordant results
under two reconstructed simulator contracts and passed exact replay,” once
the replay has actually completed.

## Relationship to the preprint

The preprint's primary plastic-heredity endpoint is F12 break-and-renewal: a
break followed later by three consecutive parent-to-selected-daughter
inheritance successes.  It requires neither episode-wide coherence nor a
stable transmitted form.  Its primary claims concern finite-horizon event
probability, prediction, intervention, and maintained local inheritance.

Strict coherent-eight is a rarer secondary endpoint.  It requires eight
successive inherited boundaries, mutual similarity among all eight selected
daughters, and separation from the old pre-break parent.  Occurrence of that
episode still does not by itself establish counterfactual lineage identity.
This follow-up asks that additional question.

Therefore, a failed lineage-identity, shared-destination, rehoming, or
two-form gate in this campaign cannot be described as a failed preprint claim
or as a general refutation of plastic heredity.  It can only narrow how the
rare strict-eight occurrence result is interpreted.  The main F12 result is
outside the tested endpoint.

## Required interpretation of the generated reports

The labels `PASS` and `FAIL` in the sealed scientific report apply only to the
follow-up's frozen exploratory gates.  They do not label claims in the
preprint.  Every summary of those reports must state all of the following:

1. the work is exploratory and reviewer-motivated;
2. the stronger claims tested are not claims made by the current preprint;
3. agreement across candidates 02 and 03 is robustness, not independent
   replication;
4. the campaign is conditional on 50 previously strict-capable rules and
   previously observed strict-B donors;
5. the rare-form rules 11, 54, and 63 are post-hoc case studies and do not
   estimate prevalence; and
6. results remain provisional until the full exact replay audit passes.

No result, number, classification, or code from `NewIdeas` is evidence in this
campaign.  That folder supplied hypotheses only.

## Safe concise wording

> In one exploratory, reviewer-motivated stress test, stronger
> lineage-identity and stable-multiform interpretations not claimed by the
> current preprint were evaluated under two reconstructed simulator
> contracts.  The contracts gave concordant results in the selected
> strict-capable cohort.  This cross-contract agreement is a robustness check,
> not an independent replication, and it does not test the preprint's primary
> F12 break-and-renewal claims.  Exact computational replay remains required
> before the exploratory result is final.
