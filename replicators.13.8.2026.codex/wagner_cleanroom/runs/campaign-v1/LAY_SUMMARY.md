This clean-room experiment asked whether a Wagner gene-regulation network can carry useful information from its present expression state into its descendants. We rebuilt the simulator independently, sampled fresh regulatory networks, and fixed all tests before examining their results. The exact-state replication finished with a **PASS** verdict across 240 independently sampled networks.

Writing one of two possible adult states into a network changed what its descendants later became. Compared with resetting the expression state, the written state changed matching-destination risk by 0.103, while the history crossover was 0.197. The current state also improved probabilistic destination prediction by 0.034 nats of log loss. These effects were judged together with persistence, shuffled-state controls, first-generation effects, split-half reliability, and exact replay—not from a single favorable number.

A separate extension asked a closer version of the preprint's prediction question: given the recent lineage history, present gene-expression pattern, and regulatory matrix, can we predict a future break followed by three faithfully inherited boundaries? Its sealed exploratory verdict was **NOT_PROMISING**. The richer model's log-loss advantage over history alone was 0.013 nats, and over a strong basin-and-sensitivity baseline it was -0.016 nats.

The result is evidence about an exact expression-state channel in Wagner networks. It does not establish that such a state arises naturally, survives a complete expression reset, or constitutes a hidden molecular carrier. The PH predictor extension is deliberately labelled exploratory and cannot rescue or alter the primary replication verdict.
